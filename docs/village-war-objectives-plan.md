# Village war campaigns

This document owns the village-war campaign rules. Sector wars retain their separate territory and garrison rules. Tyler authorized implementation on 2026-10-11: five days, a timed central shrine, one stronghold of each battle type per village, capped supporting missions, worldwide enemy PvP scoring, and Town Hall defense dispatch. The October 14 rebalance applies only to new declarations; existing wars retain their stored rules and schedule. This implementation has not been deployed.

## Schedule and victory

New village-war declarations use the campaign automatically. Existing declaration funding, one-hour preparation, peace/surrender, cooldown, winner rewards, crates and MVP settlement remain integrated with world-state. HP depletion and mercenary HP damage do not decide campaign victory.

The campaign lasts five full 24-hour days after preparation. Its winner is the village with the highest final War Points. An equal final score is a draw. Objective bonuses settle before the final comparison. A battle must start during the campaign and finish before the relevant scoring deadline. Late delivery cannot alter an already finalized result.

## Central War Shrine

One neutral sector is chosen randomly by the server and sealed at each declaration; it hosts the golden shrine for the whole war. For new version 3 declarations it opens once for two hours per campaign day, at elapsed scoring hours 2–4, 31–33, 60–62, 89–91 and 118–120. Within the five 24-hour days these are hours 2–4, 7–9, 12–14, 17–19 and 22–24. The server adds elapsed milliseconds to scoring start after the unchanged one-hour preparation; local time zones and daylight-saving transitions never shift the windows. All five opening and closing timestamps are persisted at declaration and published with the committed declaration. Town Hall and War Hall show the locked full schedule in each viewer’s local time and count down to the next stored opening or current closing. The fifth window closes exactly at the campaign deadline. A golden painted landmark appears inside that sector. Players travel there and enter the shrine during an open window; its overhead golden courtyard uses the sector artwork style, with movement tiles and the live room roster. The compact room header places Leave beside the title; metadata and countdown use a small status area, while control status and enemy actions precede expandable rules. The War Hall is an information surface and offers no remote shrine or stronghold attack buttons.

The first 10 completed human combat victories in each new window earn 20 extra points each across both sides. Both fighters must have physically entered the same golden shrine room; standing outside in its sector is insufficient. Shrine defenders cannot queue or be dispatched from town. There is no NPC shrine fallback. Entry and combat remain open until the window closes after all 10 win-point awards are used, so fighting can interrupt enemy control. Every valid shrine victory also earns normal role victory points and deducts the defeated player’s role penalty from the enemy PvP score, floored at zero. Those normal effects continue after the ten extra awards are exhausted. The existing authoritative role table is used: Kage 30 win / 50 loss, Elder 20 / 20, ANBU 15 / 0, villager 5 / 0. Roles are sealed when the combat session starts, so later appointment changes do not change a delayed result. Winner rewards, enemy deductions, the shared extra slot and the permanent receipt publish together under the existing war lock/CAS. A retry or restart cannot duplicate either side’s change. The victory’s combined normal-plus-extra award supplies the winner’s contribution credit; enemy deductions do not fabricate additional contribution. There is no separate flat worldwide award for that same match. The locked score mutation enforces the shared 10-award cap with parallel claims.

The single 100-point bonus goes to the village with the most accumulated uncontested time across the entire window. Eligible players from only one village must be inside for its timer to run. Empty and contested intervals add no time; exact ties and zero totals pay no bonus. A village can win after leaving, even if the enemy or both villages are inside at closing. Headcount never multiplies village time. Online, recovered, level-15 entrants count; exterior players do not. The server accounts for entry, departure, travel, exterior movement, battle epochs and disconnect/lease expiry, integrating intervals at exact server timestamps. Defeat ejects that exact shrine visit, and recovered players must re-enter. The presence host checkpoints accumulated totals every five seconds and at closing without client polling. Saved checkpoints survive restart; unverified offline gaps are never extrapolated. The bonus settles once and cannot be transferred by late battle claims. Its same 100 points are attributed among the winning village's verified participants in proportion to their uncontested participation time, with deterministic integer rounding; this qualifies genuine controllers for war rewards without fabricating kills or multiplying village points. Individual attribution is private. Town Hall and the room show both villages' measured control times. The bonus uses neither a supporting PvE attempt nor a human bout.

## Compatibility and rollout

Tyler approved reuse of the existing persisted campaign version on October 14. Retained version 2 campaigns keep 100 points per shrine human victory, their stored shared 15-win cap, their 1,000-point accumulated-time bonus, and their original stored opening/closing timestamps (normally hours 20–24, 44–48, 68–72, 92–96 and 116–120). Only new declarations write version 3 with the rotating schedule, shared ten-extra-win cap and ongoing normal shrine scoring. Version 2 shrine fights retain their original objective-only behavior, including no normal reward or enemy deduction after the cap. The October 15 clarification treats the 300-point pool as extra rewards only; version 3 remains the approved, unshipped declaration version. Scoring, delayed results, bonus settlement and proportional participant attribution resolve rewards from the persisted version. Views and restart recovery never regenerate or convert an existing schedule. No SQL migration, row backfill or new KV key is required. Deploy backend and rebuilt client together and reload clients left open across the release so they recognize version 3 campaigns; do not roll back to a server that only understands version 2 after version 3 declarations exist.

Stronghold rewards, intel thresholds, personal mission allowances, the effective support ceiling, worldwide PvP, preparation duration, mercenaries and other economies are unchanged. The existing locks/CAS settlement, permanent zero-point receipts, exact ties and no-offline-gap control checkpoints remain authoritative. Configured point budgets are arithmetic, not evidence of improved participation or measured balance.

## Sector strongholds

Each village has three distinct randomly selected home-sector strongholds, excluding its village gate. Exactly one is shinobi combat, one pet battle, and one Chronicle card battle. Nominations are sealed at declaration. Participants see their own defenses immediately; enemy locations are private until revealed by shared reconnaissance.

Players enter a discovered enemy stronghold in its sector and attack its ANBU defense. The server checks current sector presence again when admitting an assault, so a cached interior cannot authorize an attack after traveling away. A queued, online, available enemy player takes the defense in the nominated mode. With no eligible queued player, the server randomly selects an enemy ANBU member who can supply that specialty's sealed combat snapshot, pet team or legal card deck; the seated Kage remains a reserve. Tyler confirmed ANBU availability and repeat use on 2026-10-12. Async defenders are reusable masked copies: the real member need not be online or healthy, combat copies start at full strength, and pet copies can fight while the real pets breed or go on expeditions. These fights do not change the real defender's save or activities. Repeated selection does not consume a member or impose a defender cooldown; only the attacker's personal support limits apply. Sector-war defender rotation remains separate.

Tyler clarified that strong combat AI means smart play using the selected player's actual build. Each new assault seals the member's current authoritative player combat stats, learned/equipped moves, armor, bloodline, weapon and derived gear effects through the same hydrator as player combat. It does not reuse the daily vault snapshot, scale to the attacker, substitute a generic NPC kit or apply boss/difficulty stat bonuses. The full-strength copy uses the existing tactical planner at maximum decision competence for legal combinations, counters, healing, movement and resource/cooldown management. Only its masked name/portrait and AI decision metadata differ from the player's sealed build. An already-started assault keeps its original copy when the member later changes equipment.

The physical entry prompt identifies the nominated combat/pet/card specialty, distinguishes the 50-point human award from supporting ANBU rewards, and opens the war stronghold. Ordinary strongholds retain their separate chamber-exploration and supply-raid instructions.

Each stronghold has three scored human victories available across the full campaign. A human win earns 50 points; the village with more wins earns a 150-point completion bonus. The bonus settles when all slots are scored or the campaign ends. NPC fallback wins grant supporting PvE credit and consume the Sabotage allowance, without consuming human objective slots or granting objective bonuses.

Ordinary stronghold patrol/ANBU routes cannot bypass the nominated mode during a campaign. World combat inside a pet/card objective is refused. Leaving the objective clears the interior location; the ordinary stronghold experience returns outside campaigns.

## Supporting operations and discovery

Field orders replace the former three identically named patrol fights. Tyler authorized distinct travel missions and a Town Hall War tab on 2026-10-12. One player can hold one active field order; acceptance reserves its attempt before any travel. Client actions bind that order's ID, so a stale tab cannot act on a newer order. Completed steps are durable campaign admissions; elapsed hold time is server-process evidence and restarts after lost presence, an expired progress lease, interruption or server restart. Rewards and combat outcomes remain server-authoritative.

| Order | Objective | Success reward |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supply | Collect a sealed package by standing still outside at your own village outskirts for 10 seconds. Carry it to one server-assigned, currently allied-controlled field sector and stand still outside for a 20-second handoff. | 10 raw support points + 1 intel |
| Sabotage | Visit any three distinct sectors from the enemy-controlled route list, including enemy village outskirts. Stand still outside for 20 continuous seconds in each; repeated visits to the same sector cannot count twice. Ownership is rechecked on each progress request. | 20 raw support points + 1 intel |
| Espionage | Travel to one assigned enemy-controlled ordinary stronghold, physically enter it and defeat its AI sentry or an enemy player inside. The order uses a non-nominated stronghold so it cannot bypass a pet/card specialty or reveal a secret objective assignment. The real player battle seals both fighters' interior presence at creation. | 20 raw support points + 2 intel |

Supply's route and timing are implementation defaults chosen for Tyler's delegated supply-design request. The package is sealed mission state, not a tradeable inventory item or treasury expense. Movement, travel, entering an interior, combat and a progress gap over 10 seconds reset an unfinished stationary hold. Polling only runs on visible field/stronghold screens; server timers cannot be completed from Town Hall or a client timestamp. An AI sentry loss/abandonment fails Espionage; an unfinished sentry battle must settle before changing its order. An enemy-player espionage win also retains its ordinary world-PvP award, while its separate support award obeys the support ceiling. A generation-bound receipt prevents repeated mission credit.

The village-shared intel meter identifies one enemy stronghold at each 24/48/72 threshold. A reveal includes its sector, combat/pet/card specialty, scored-battle progress and objective scoring availability. Own objectives are always known. Intel does not reveal player locations, secret decks, pet teams or unpublished enemy assignments.

During an active campaign, recovered level-15 villagers can physically enter their currently owned ordinary non-gate strongholds from the World Map for **war guard duty**, matching the other campaign participation requirements. The separate guard entrance shows enemy visitors and dispatches ordinary world PvP; it charges no mission attempt and provides no patrol, cache, treasury or friendly Anbu rewards. Ownership, campaign timing and verified sector presence are checked by the server. Guard refresh requires an existing visit; a stale tab's leave cannot eject a newer visit. War interior visits bind movement, travel/resource epoch and connection identity. Exterior walking, travel round trips and reconnection require fresh entry; the authoritative in-battle epoch can retain the admitted room while fighting, but the old exterior view cannot inherit membership after combat releases. Nominated combat/pet/card objectives retain their mode-specific Town Hall queues and never receive this ordinary entrance. Outside war guard duty, ordinary infiltration retains its level-100 requirement. Espionage attackers need level 15 and may choose the scaled sentry when no enemy player is present. Espionage assignments exclude village gates, which have village entrances rather than stronghold sites; an impossible route is rejected before charging an attempt.

Caps reset at each campaign-day boundary and charge an **attempt at admission**, including losses or abandonment:

| Allowance | Per campaign day |
| --- | ---: |
| Player, all operations | 4 |
| Player, Supply | 1 |
| Player, Sabotage / Espionage, each | 2 |

Tyler reduced Supply's support reward by half on 2026-10-12 and subsequently removed all village-wide mission limits and quadrupled discovery requirements. Supply pays 10 points with one personal attempt per campaign day; Sabotage and Espionage each allow two personal attempts, within four total personal attempts. Intel remains village-shared and reveals targets at 24, 48 and 72; mission intel rewards remain 1/1/2. No villager's admissions consume another player's allowance. ANBU fallback assaults share that player's Sabotage caps. Reconnection resumes an existing admitted order/battle without charging another attempt, and abandoned orders cannot be refunded. An unfinished order may complete later but spends its admission day's allowance.

Participation can now increase the village's raw support and discovery rate. One player can earn at most six intel daily (30 across five days); a solo participant cannot reveal all three targets through missions alone. In the all-success, intel-priority audit scenario, 1/2/3/10/50 participants reach full discovery on no day/no day/day four/day two/day one respectively. This is configured arithmetic, not production telemetry. The existing 20% effective-support ceiling still requires PvP scoring. The Town Hall War tab displays only personal total and per-type attempts, the exact campaign-day reset, the 72-intel meter, discovered targets, shrine windows and defense queues. Its sector-war display retains the separate Sector Map controls. The World Map retains the compact order tracker and physical espionage entrance at the assigned sector.

Effective support score is `min(raw support, floor(PvP score / 4))`. Thus PvE contributes at most 20% of the final total, and PvE alone cannot win a campaign. Excess earned support remains visible and becomes effective as PvP score increases. HP-damage mercenary bands cannot be hired for a score campaign; sector-war mercenaries remain available under their existing rules.

## Shared war economy and settlement

Temporary preparations belong to one funded declaration instance. Campaign Watchtower, Barracks and War Academy have three tiers, costing 40 / 80 / 120 WR (240 WR to complete one). Purchase requires an explicit instance and target tier; the exact war row is locked before the village war chest and rechecked before spending. Debit and grant share one war-chest write, including lost-answer replays. Rematches and simultaneous contests cannot inherit levels. Ended, surrendered, unfunded and superseded wars cannot receive upgrades; their preparations disappear from the live view and are removed before daily upkeep. Sector Watchtower stops at tier one (40 WR), because sector wars use its attack reports but have no campaign Espionage orders; the API and UI reject useless additional tiers. Historical village-wide combat-building levels grant no points or discounts. New Ramparts purchases remain retired without spending resources.

| Building | Current effect | Lifetime |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Watchtower | Private reports of admitted attacks on own campaign strongholds and contested-sector Combat/Pet/Card battles. Campaigns also give +1/+2/+3 intel on each player's first successful Espionage order each campaign day. | One war instance, three tiers |
| Barracks | 10/20/30% less temporary-building upkeep for this instance. Defending Combat sector wars also get 5/10/15% cheaper mercenary hires; quotes and debits use the same instance. Hire limits remain unchanged. | One war instance, three tiers |
| War Academy | +10/20/30% raw support points on field missions and supporting ANBU victories. Sector player-run Combat/Pet/Card garrison victories receive the bonus after normal half-weight, within the existing war-wide cap. Unfed sector participants receive half the additional Academy bonus. | One war instance, three tiers |
| Supply Depot | +5 WR per village daily per level, max +50, separately from the unchanged 25 WR per held sector. Material conversion stays 100 + 30 WR/day per level at 10 materials per WR. | Permanent, ten levels |
| Treasury Vault | 3% relative occupation-tax reduction per level, max 30%. | Permanent, ten levels |

Temporary upkeep is `ceil(2 × total tiers × (1 − 0.1 × Barracks tier))` per instance; all three maxed cost 13 WR/day. Permanent upkeep and seal/material prices remain unchanged. Dormancy suspends bonuses. Mission tiers are sealed at admission, so upgrades cannot retroactively change an accepted order. Watchtower's first-success opportunity is consumed even at tier zero; losses and abandoned orders do not use it. Intel stays bounded at 72 and reveals at 24/48/72. Academy changes raw support only; `min(rawSupport, floor(PvP/4))` still governs effective campaign PvE. Tier-three Supply pays 13 raw points, and Sabotage/Espionage/ANBU support pay 26. There is no Ryo bonus, extra personal attempt, village-wide mission cap, extra scored objective slot, NPC stat buff or change to shrine time scoring.

Town Hall → War and the Sector Map show an individual-war selector, tier cards, live WR balance, effects, expiry and private Watchtower reports. Town Hall → Upgrades shows only Depot and Vault as permanent war infrastructure. Sector mercenary management is also available in the War tab, with prices projected separately for each contest. Field-order cards show upgraded rewards, and completion summaries use durable awarded points and actual intel added.

A funded declaration costs the existing 800 WR before the held-sector comeback discount and retains one hour of preparation. Both villages reserve exclusively against concurrent village or sector wars. A repeated active declaration cannot charge again. Sector mercenary purchases lock the serving war instance before the war chest, recheck its live generation and deadline, and refuse a quote whose war has ended or changed. Combat sector defense retains its three-band shared hire allowance; campaigns use human defenders and reusable ANBU copies instead.

Final effective points determine the campaign winner; HP cannot override them. Existing generation-bound spoils, village win/loss standings, MVPs and contribution-based reward eligibility remain integrated. Draws add neither a win nor a loss. Player `warsWon` increments when their earned victory crate is claimed, rather than automatically for every member of a winning village. Victory-crate and combined reward routes share claim markers, so calling both cannot duplicate that count. When Legacy is enabled, those routes also share the durable war-win deed; the combined route delivers the earned MVP deed once. Failed deed delivery can be retried without another reward grant, and a fresh Legacy baseline excludes both new counter increments before applying them. MVP claims accept the same authenticated account whether a contribution writer recorded its slug or display name. Supporting mission packages and support points are separate from treasury WR, provisions and materials.

The browser's combined-reward claim cache is scoped to the declaration generation, with the ended timestamp retained as the historical-row fallback. A granted or temporarily no-grant predecessor cannot suppress a same-pair rematch's rewards in an open session; requests still use the authoritative canonical pair ID.

## Worldwide PvP and defense queues

Sanctioned victories over the opposing village anywhere in the world grant 20 points. Spars, ranked matches, admin/test sessions and battles without real-player progression authority are excluded. A stronghold-bound match grants its objective award once rather than also receiving worldwide credit. Version 3 shrine matches instead combine their normal role award and enemy deduction with any remaining extra shrine award, as specified above; they do not also receive the flat 20-point worldwide award. Ordinary world matches retain their existing flat 20-point campaign scoring without enemy deductions.

Level-15, recovered players can queue at Town Hall for their chosen stronghold objective. Pet defenders need an eligible carried team; card defenders need a legal deck. Queues last up to 24 hours and renew while the panel is open. Dispatch rechecks the defender's current level, village, recovery, online availability, travel and battle state; eligibility at queue time cannot substitute for eligibility when attacked. One unfinished campaign battle prevents another assignment. Shrine opponents are selected from players physically inside the same shrine room; strongholds can field an ANBU fallback.

Combat dispatch uses an ordinary shared PvP session, sealed objective authority and battle notifications. Pet battles use the existing deterministic Showdown war-team resolver and replay. Card battles use the existing authoritative Chronicle engine, including its turn timeout and server forfeit. A card defense triggers an actionable heartbeat notice, including when the defender has left Town Hall. The defender can resume pet/card assignments from the war or Town Hall panel.

Combat completion is reconciled under the campaign run lock before another attack or defense dispatch, including reconnects that never reopen the War Hall. Drawn combats release their objective reservations without points or a scored bout. A player with pending PvP settlement must acknowledge it before another attack or defense assignment. An open Town Hall defense panel follows the current war and clears expired objectives. Objective cards use the combat/pet/card illustrations and gold sector-style shrine artwork in `shinobij.client/public/ui/village-war/`; provenance and exact prompts are recorded in its manifest. The follow-up integration audit is in `docs/village-war-integration-audit.md`.

Completion time determines battle-point eligibility. Accepted delayed battle results can update eligible win points while the war remains active, but cannot change the shrine's settled uncontested-time bonus or a finalized war result. Stronghold battle-leader bonuses retain their existing late-result correction.
