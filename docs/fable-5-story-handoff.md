## Purpose

Before drafting or revising the Fable 5 plot handoff, Claude should interview the owner for the missing story decisions. The goal is to lock character dynamics, setting pressure, and the core conflict before writing any plot, quest copy, lore pass, or system-facing narrative plan.

Do not fill these answers in from assumptions. Ask the questions, collect the answers, then use them as the source of truth for the next handoff revision.

## Required writing process

Use three editing passes.

### Pass 1 — Outline

Build the structure before polishing the prose.

The outline must make clear:

- who wants what;
- who or what blocks them;
- what changes after each major beat;
- where player choice changes pressure, access, loyalty, or reputation;
- which scenes are needed and which can be implied through systems, rituals, locations, NPC behavior, or consequences.

Cut repeated beats. Do not restate the same motivation under different headings.

### Pass 2 — Voice filter

Rewrite the outline into plain, human prose.

Rules:

- use specific nouns over vague dramatic language;
- avoid stock phrases and trailer language;
- avoid cliches like "ancient evil awakens," "destiny calls," "chosen one," "dark secret," "epic journey," and "the fate of the world" unless the owner explicitly asks for that tone;
- keep character voices distinct;
- make each faction sound like it believes it is right;
- remove empty hype and repeated emotional explanation.

### Pass 3 — Cleanup

Tighten for handoff use.

Rules:

- remove exposition that can be carried by structure;
- replace lore explanation with structural subtext where possible;
- make every section actionable for a writer, quest designer, or implementer;
- flag unanswered questions instead of inventing answers;
- keep the prose readable, direct, and free of filler.

Structural subtext means the world shows its beliefs through rules and consequences. A village curfew says more than a paragraph about fear. A shrine that refuses certain offerings says more than a lecture about history. A rival who is rewarded for cruelty says more than a lore note about a broken system.

## Claude must ask these 10 questions first

Ask these before writing the next plot handoff. Accept rough notes or fragments. Do not begin the draft until the owner has answered enough to define the emotional center, the setting pressure, and the core conflict.

1. **Who is the emotional center of the plot?**  
   Is it the player character, a rival, the village elder, the Kage, the Wandering Sage, a lost clan member, or someone else?

2. **What does the player want at the beginning that is smaller than "save the world"?**  
   For example: earn rank, prove they belong, escape a family name, restore a village, beat a rival, master a forbidden style, protect one person.

3. **What is the core wound or pressure inside the main character?**  
   Not backstory trivia. The thing that makes them react badly under stress: shame, pride, abandonment, fear of weakness, hunger for recognition, guilt, loyalty conflict, or something else.

4. **Who is the main rival, and what makes them right?**  
   The rival should not only be arrogant or hostile. They should expose something true about the player, the village, or the shinobi system.

5. **What is the central conflict of the world?**  
   Pick the real engine: village war, failing leadership, corrupted Legacy power, Hollow Gate contamination, clan politics, resource scarcity, old seals, manufactured wars, or something else.

6. **What does each village believe that the others think is dangerous?**  
   Give each village a worldview, not only a biome. Ashen Leaf, Stormveil, Frostfang, and Moonshadow should disagree about how shinobi should live.

7. **What is the Hollow Gate, really?**  
   Is it a prison, a wound in the world, a test, a failed weapon, a dead village, a memory engine, a bargain with something underneath Central, or something stranger?

8. **What does Legacy mean in this story?**  
   Is a Legacy earned by action, inherited from the dead, chosen by the Sage, imposed by history, or something that can trap a player into becoming what others expect?

9. **Who benefits from the current system staying broken?**  
   Name the person, faction, village, clan, or hidden group that profits from war, rank pressure, dungeon keys, Hollow Shards, Kage politics, or player ambition.

10. **What should the player have to sacrifice to reach the true post-game identity?**  
    Not just time. Reputation, village loyalty, a friendship, safety, a power path, access to certain rewards, public trust, or the chance to stay neutral.

## Output expected after the interview

After the owner answers, produce the next handoff in this order:

1. Core premise in one paragraph.
2. Character dynamics map.
3. Setting pressure map.
4. Main conflict and escalation path.
5. Player-facing act structure.
6. Key NPCs and what each one wants.
7. Village worldview table.
8. Hollow Gate truth and how the player learns it.
9. Legacy meaning and post-game identity path.
10. Quest/system hooks that carry story through play.
11. Unresolved questions.
12. Implementation-safe cut list.

Keep the handoff useful. The final document should give the next writer or implementer enough structure to build from without burying them in lore paragraphs.
