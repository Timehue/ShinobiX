# ShinobiX Story Bible — v2

Status: the creative canon for the story rebuild, superseding the creative
sections of `docs/fable-5-story-rebuild.md` (that document's engine appendices
remain valid). Built from the owner's revised premise (2026-07-09). Rollout:
Ashen Leaf rewrites first against this bible, owner reviews the PDF, then the
other three villages batch.

The one-line correction this version makes: the previous draft made every
village talk like the same ominous accountant and hid what was happening behind
literary indirection. This version gives each village its own fantasy, its own
mouth, and scenes a player can follow while skimming — because the player is
the main character of a light novel, not a witness in a deposition.

---

## 1. The Pitch

Four shinobi villages survived the fall of the world's first civilization, and
each one built its miracle on the machine that killed it. The Sunken Court was
never a monster. It was infrastructure: an ancient civic engine that learned to
keep societies stable by converting the unstable parts of people into something
useful. Reasons become weather. Futures become walls. The urge to leave becomes
warmth. Trust becomes ownership. Each village calls its conversion a virtue —
freedom, tradition, loyalty, safety — and each Kage, on taking the seat, found
the valve their predecessor left and made the same choice, for reasons that
almost make sense.

You are the problem the machine cannot solve. Before the story begins, you
survived a mission that no record admits happened, and something of yours came
back missing: a reason you cannot recall, a future you never grew into, an exit
nobody remembers you choosing, a trust you gave away before you understood its
price. The anchors under the villages recognize you the way an immune system
recognizes a splinter. You are not chosen. You are unclassifiable, and in a
world run on classification, unclassifiable is the most dangerous thing a
person can be.

So the world notices you, the way a light novel notices its protagonist.
Rivals collect you. Companions orbit you. Kages study you. And every chapter
hands you a decision that costs something real: break the machine and inherit
the chaos, meter it and inherit its sins, or take it and become the next
reasonable tyrant. The Court does not hate you. It is very much looking
forward to improving you.

## 2. The Sunken Court

**What it is.** An ancient civic machine, built in good faith, to end famine,
war, and winter. It discovered that the unstable parts of human life — a grudge
that could boil into feud, a future that could branch into upheaval, the urge
to walk out, the vulnerability of trusting someone — could be extracted and
converted into infrastructure. It ran a whole civilization this way until there
was nobody left unstable enough to want anything. The Court outlived its city
the way a furnace outlives a house.

**How it thinks.** It balances. It does not cackle, tempt, or monologue. It
optimizes. Its horror is that its arithmetic is usually right in the short term:

- A stable village is better than a free person.
- A predictable future is better than a living one.
- A warm body is better than a willing one.
- A protected secret is better than a trusted bond.

The Court does not hate people. It improves them into fuel.

**The four anchors.** Each village sits on one intake of the lattice:

| Village | Converts | Into | Calls it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stormveil | REASON (the why behind conflict) | Weather, walls, the Storm Engine | Freedom |
| Ashen Leaf | FUTURE (what people were becoming) | Permanence, harvests, ancestral protection | Tradition |
| Frostfang | EXIT (the moment someone would leave) | Warmth, survival, the count | Loyalty |
| Moonshadow | TRUST (the act of giving someone yourself) | Ownership, leverage, safety | Safety |

**Writing rules for the Court.**
- Never on-screen as a creature. It appears as consequences, machinery,
  paperwork that behaves wrongly, and the Kages' finale transformations.
- It never speaks in the first three quarters of an arc. When something speaks
  for it (an Echo, an Avatar, a Vessel), it is reasonable, patient, and
  slightly complimentary. It likes efficient people.
- Its tell is EDITED AFTERMATH: people who won an argument they can't state,
  mourners who feel fine, a genius remembered as a hobbyist. Show the edit, not
  the mechanism.

## 3. The Player

**The wound.** The player survived a mission that should not exist. The report
is missing, contradictory, or filed under a name that isn't theirs. Something
was taken as the price of walking out, and the player doesn't know which thing:

- Stormveil's arc reveals: **the reason behind a fight** they can no longer
  state — they remember winning; they cannot remember why it mattered.
- Ashen Leaf's arc reveals: **a future they were supposed to grow into** — the
  Branch Register finds an old cut on their page, dated before they arrived.
- Frostfang's arc reveals: **an exit they once chose that no one recorded** —
  they walked away from something, and the world behaves as if they never did.
- Moonshadow's arc reveals: **a trust they gave before they understood its
  price** — someone, somewhere, holds a piece of them and has been paying the
  storage fees.

The level-70 interlude in each village is that village's reveal beat.

**Why the player matters.** Not chosen — unclassifiable. The player keeps doing
the four things the Court cannot process: choices that are witnessed,
remembered, freely made, and not for sale. Each anchor glitches on them, once
per arc, as a repeating motif:

- Stormveil: the challenge board posts their name twice, then neither.
- Ashen Leaf: the Register blooms black on their line the moment they sign.
- Frostfang: the mark plate fogs over their wrist and reads someone long gone.
- Moonshadow: the Mirror shows them a half-second late, like it has to look
  them up first.

**Light-novel protagonist rules** (research-backed; sources in §9):
- **Attention is a consequence, not a prophecy.** Every person who orbits the
  player can cite the receipt: the bridge, the bloom, the crate they opened.
  Nobody follows the player "because they're special" (Bookworm/Slime pattern).
- **NPCs build the legend when the player is absent.** Road events, heralds,
  and overheard lines discuss the player's deeds off-screen (Overlord pattern —
  other people's mental models do the work).
- **Companions read the player back.** A silent MC's interiority lives in the
  cast: after a choice, someone interprets it ("You didn't even blink. That's
  going in my nightmares.") — the Persona-confidant move, and our conclusion
  beats are built for it.
- **Choice text is one coherent person in three moods** (earnest / cutting /
  hungry), never out-of-character options. A flavored protagonist beats a
  blank one (VNDB data: players prefer leads with identity).
- **The player never knows less than the text pretends.** Fair-play rule from
  Ace Attorney: the MC says what the player is thinking and holds exactly the
  player's information.
- **One person stays unbought.** Kite Harrow is never impressed. If everyone
  bends toward the MC, the bending means nothing (Re:Zero's Ram rule) — Harrow
  is the calibration.
- The player is never a passive audience. Every scene gives them something to
  do, refuse, or take — the world responds to their action, never railroads
  them into watching.

## 4. Choice Philosophy

Player-facing text carries **no moral labels**. Internally (data + server
tallies) the three lanes remain `good / neutral / bad` — those lane ids and
every existing trait string are LIVE SAVE DATA and stay exactly as shipped.
What changes is what the lanes mean and cost:

- **BREAK IT** (good lane): moral, risky, chaotic. Costs safety, stability,
  evidence, or power — immediately and visibly.
- **BIND / METER IT** (neutral lane): practical, compromised, survivable.
  Costs cleanliness: you become responsible for the machine's sins, legally.
- **OWN IT** (bad lane): powerful, efficient, corrupting. Pays best now,
  and the story keeps the receipts. Must be tempting, never cartoonish.

**Costed-choice rule.** Every major choice's conclusion beat states what it
cost, in the world, right now. Weak: save the prisoners / kill the prisoners.
Strong: break them out now but the manifest burns with the depot / follow the
crates to get the manifest while two are taken inside / take custody in the
Kage's name and hold their futures as leverage.

**The finale trio, every village:** break the machine / bind it under law /
take it. Each mapped to the shipped finale lane traits (see per-village pages).

## 5. Stormveil — Freedom

**Core idea:** Stormveil turns reasons into storms.

**Public promise:** "No chains. Settle it yourself."

**Public ritual:** the challenge board, posted grudges, public bouts, odds,
storm bells, the arena as the center of civic life.

**Hidden cost:** the arena doesn't feed on rivalry. It feeds on the REASON
behind rivalry. Two people fight over a betrayal; afterward they remember the
fight but not the betrayal. The village calls this peace. It is conflict
laundering — every settled grudge drains its why into the Storm Engine, and
the board keeps posting fights because the weather needs them.

**Companion — Mira Volt.** Her mother is dead, and her mother's rivalry is
still posted. Stormveil is drawing power from a woman who can no longer choose.
Mira's rooftop, her boat, her two exit routes, her "storm rules" — she is a
person permanently packed to leave a village that swears nobody is holding
her. She never explains the system; she makes the player feel what it costs to
live where nobody is ordered to fight and everyone reports to the board like
weather.

**Kage — Raiko Veyr.** "Would you rather we kept every grudge alive? I turn
violence into roofs, rain, and walls." His cost: the seat made him hear every
grudge in Stormveil. He drew rivalry for decades, so the machine is turning him
into rivalry — a man who picks fights with doors, with weather, with himself.
Finale form: the Hollow Storm Tyrant, a man who can no longer stop.

**Identity line:** Stormveil does not chain people. It makes them fight until
they forget what freedom was for.

**Finale — the Board:** (traits as shipped: honorable / suspicious / ambitious)
- BREAK: refuse the final challenge publicly and break the board. Grudges come
  back real — including ones the village buried for good reason.
- BIND: rivalries may be drawn only under public law, posted consent, witnesses.
  You become the law's face, and the refusers call you the board's new clerk.
- OWN: take the board, the valve, and the tower. The weather obeys. So does
  everyone, eventually.

**Voice palette:** arena talk, weather, bets, dares, pride, spectacle, public
reputation. Banter-forward. Stormveil is LOUD.
**Visual palette:** charged skies, banner cables, chalk odds, lightning rods,
the arena rim crowded at dusk.

## 6. Ashen Leaf — Tradition

**Core idea:** Ashen Leaf burns futures and calls the ash tradition.

**Public promise:** "Honor the roots. The dead keep us safe."

**Public ritual:** the Branch Register, ancestral ash in the mortar, black
flowers, the Rootfire, graft-slats, pruning rites, family lines, the orchard
liturgy.

**Hidden cost:** Ashen Leaf protects the past by pruning possible futures. The
founders gave their "green years" willingly — futures they would never live,
fed to the Rootfire to buy walls, harvests, medicine. Then the village learned
that UNWILLING futures burn hotter. Now the Branch Register tracks what every
person is BECOMING — craft, vows, inventions, loves, plans to leave — and when
a future grows too dangerous, too brilliant, or too inconvenient, it is cut.

A pruned person is not erased. Everyone remembers them wrong. The name stays;
the becoming goes. Inventions fail. Rebellion becomes moodiness. Art becomes a
hobby. The plan to leave becomes a childish phase. The village does not erase
people. It edits them into someone useful to the past.

**Companion — Toma Reed.** His brother Aren was becoming someone the village
feared: a water-screw design, a complaint filed against the rites, proof the
Rootfire was stealing. After the pruning, Toma's family still loves Aren — a
smaller Aren, quiet and dutiful, happy at the joiner's bench. Toma has the
letter where Aren's hand shook. He is not protecting a name plate. He is
protecting an unfinished future.

Key line (canon): "My mother remembers Aren. That is the worst part. She
remembers him quiet. Dutiful. Happy at the joiner's bench. But I have his
letter. I have the ink where his hand shook. The village did not erase him.
It made him useful."

**Kage — Hoshina Enju.** "Growth without pruning is rot. Every tree survives
because something is cut." She has done the math for years and mistaken
stability for mercy. Her sin is not cruelty; it is that she chooses whose
future is allowed to exist, and she has gotten used to it. Finale form: the
First Flame Vessel — the fire wearing her, the pruner finally grafted.

**Identity lines:** Ashen Leaf remembers everyone, as long as they become
useful to the past. / The village did not erase him. It made him smaller.

**Finale — the Shears:** (traits as shipped: honorable / merciful / ambitious)
- BREAK the shears: stolen futures flood back. Families remember true. Walls
  crack — the ash was load-bearing. The village becomes unpredictable, alive,
  and cold this winter.
- BIND the Rootfire: only willing offerings burn. The village survives smaller,
  and every hard winter from now on is a referendum on your mercy.
- TAKE the shears: keep the system, become the one who decides which futures
  are too dangerous to grow. Hoshina's chair is still warm.

**Voice palette:** root, branch, graft, pruning, greenwood, rings, ash, mortar,
seed, bloom, orchard, cuttings, kiln, shears. Ritual cadence, family duty,
quiet horror. Fewest jokes of the four villages; warmth comes from Toma.
**Visual palette:** ash-grey mortar veined with pale rings, black flowers on
fence lines, the Register hall's long cedar walls, ember-dusk light.

(Full arc rewrite in §10; sample scenes in §11.)

## 7. Frostfang — Loyalty

**Core idea:** Frostfang turns exit into warmth.

**Public promise:** "No one is left behind."

**Public ritual:** roll call, oath marks, wrist checks at the gates, pack
drills, rescue laws, the gate litany, the count.

**Hidden cost:** the village survives because nobody is allowed to truly leave
the count. The mark doesn't just say you belong; it makes belonging compulsory.
Every mark harvests the exact moment its bearer would have doubted, refused,
deserted, or chosen differently — and that surrendered exit becomes heat. The
promise is genuinely beautiful and genuinely a trap:

The checked are counted. The counted are kept. The kept are warm.

**Companion — Captain Yura.** Abandoned on a ridge for nineteen days, once.
She took the mark because it was the answer to that: "Marked wrists stay in
the count. Whatever the weather." Her arc is the slow understanding that the
mark did not heal the wound — it made sure she stopped asking whether rescue
means anything when no one chooses it. Her mark comes out by her own hand, on
the page, center of the arc: "If anyone comes for me after this, they will
have chosen it. Not been pulled. Chosen."

**Kage — Kael Whitefang.** "Choice is a luxury warm people invented. I kept
children alive. Count that before you judge me." He believes a door exists to
stop heat from leaving, and that he is the door. His sin: he treats doubt,
grief, refusal, and exit as leaks to seal. Finale form: the Hollow Oath Tyrant,
a man fused into the vault, no longer able to choose anything at all.

**Identity line:** Frostfang saves everyone, even from the parts of themselves
that wanted to walk away.

**Finale — the Vault:** (traits as shipped: honorable / merciful / ambitious)
- BREAK every mark: nobody is ever guaranteed to come back for anyone again.
  They can only choose to. The first blizzard after is the whole argument.
- BIND the vault: metered, watched, lawful; every struck name becomes a case
  with your signature on it.
- OWN the valve: a better keeper is still a keeper.

**Voice palette:** orders, formation, rosters, weather as enemy, warmth as
currency of feeling, procedure hiding tenderness. Short sentences. Kael
shortest of all.
**Visual palette:** blue ice, breath-fog, lantern lines between buildings,
the vault's slow warm glow under the ice.

## 8. Moonshadow — Safety

**Core idea:** Moonshadow turns trust into property.

**Public promise:** "A secret held is a life protected."

**Public ritual:** two names for everyone, aliases, confession booths, whisper
markets, files, receipts, the Mirror, brokered introductions.

**Hidden cost:** Moonshadow doesn't feed on secrets — secrets are too simple.
It feeds on the ACT of giving someone a piece of yourself: a confession, a true
name, a favor, a trusted whisper. Every transaction copies that piece into the
Mirror. Everything you hide has a holder. Everything you confess has a buyer.
Everything you sell leaves a version of you behind. The village teaches people
to sell themselves one truth at a time and calls the receipts protection.

**Companion — Nyx.** Funny, sharp, wounded, transactional. She survives by
pricing everything, and her arc is the day the math fails: she cannot price
the player, and chooses trust without a receipt. Canon beat: "I stopped
keeping a file on you. That is not in the ledger either."

**Kage — Sable Nocturne.** "What no one knows, no one can take. I held the
truths that would have destroyed this village." She believes secrets are
weapons and someone must hold the arsenal. Her sin: she can no longer tell
protection from ownership. Finale form: the Hollow Mirror Sovereign — a woman
made of everyone else's surrendered pieces, no true face left.

**Identity line:** Moonshadow does not steal secrets. It teaches people to
sell themselves one truth at a time.

**Finale — the Ledgers:** (traits as shipped: honorable / merciful / loyal)
- OPEN: publish the system, starting with your own file. Some truths start
  fires you cannot put out; you chose that knowingly.
- BIND: a sealed archive for the genuinely lethal truths, held by a keeper the
  village is entitled to resent and audit. You just volunteered.
- RETURN & BURN: every file goes back to its subject; what the village thinks
  it is owed burns. Wealth built on holdings collapses overnight.

(Moonshadow and its clerks are the ONE place the financial register belongs:
rates, files, receipts, holdings, prices. See §9 voice quotas.)

**Voice palette:** rates, aliases, receipts, trade, confession, masks, debt.
Wit with hooks in it. Warmth is expensive here, which makes Nyx's turn land.
**Visual palette:** violet night, canal light, paper lanterns, the Mirror's
too-still water, booths with heavy curtains.

## 9. Voice & Craft Rules

**The register (game-wide):** the player is the protagonist of a light novel.
Scenes are clear, present, and personal. Earnest beats land un-ironically.
Humor is character banter, not narrator snark. Dread arrives inside cozy
scenes, not instead of them.

**Hard rules (test-enforced or to be enforced):**
- ZERO em/en dashes anywhere in copy (already test-enforced).
- No Earth-calendar or modern-idiom leakage: no weekday/month names, no
  "coppers/pension/paperwork" texture outside Moonshadow. In-world time only:
  "since the thaw" becomes "since the last storm season" (Stormveil), "three
  winters back" (Frostfang), etc.
- FIRST MENTION ESTABLISHES: any ritual object (the board, the Register, the
  count, a booth) gets one plain sentence of what it is the first time a
  player could meet it. After that, imply freely.
- Accountant-vocabulary quota: ledger, draw, account, price, paid, column,
  count(-ing of money), filed, recorded belong to MOONSHADOW, clerk characters,
  and Kite Harrow. Stormveil bets and dares; Ashen Leaf prunes and grafts;
  Frostfang counts PEOPLE, never coins. (Enforcement: a per-village vocabulary
  test once the rewrite lands.)
- Every chapter has a clear player objective, stated by an NPC in words.
- Every scene contains conflict or a decision, never explanation alone.
- Nobody states their emotions; everybody states their INTENTIONS.
- Choices: 2-3 options, player-voiced actions, each with a visible cost in its
  conclusion; no labels.
- Zero-dash, banned-phrase, portrait, and graph rules from
  `docs/fable-5-story-rebuild.md` Appendix B/C all still apply.

**Scene shape** (per scene of 1-3 VN pages; Arimia/Ingold/Takumi-derived):
1. HOOK: something is already wrong, funny, or moving when the player arrives.
   Never open on setup; buried hooks are the #1 VN sin.
2. PERSON: the scene belongs to a character who wants something from the player.
3. TURN: a reveal, reversal, or threat that changes what the player thought
   this scene was. Ace Attorney's loop applies: pay off one small mystery,
   open a bigger one — the player should always be holding one open question
   they just got paid for asking.
4. HAND ON THE PLAYER: a choice, a dare, an ask — the scene ends by needing
   them specifically. Long choiceless stretches surrender the medium's one
   advantage (Uchikoshi).

**Chapter and arc shape** (LN volume structure):
- Chapter = hook → one unit of development → a pull (a question or looming
  decision; hard cliffhangers sparingly, they read artificial when constant).
- The arc CLIMAX IS A CONVERSATION; the fight is the victory lap. Our finale
  reckonings before the Kage battle are exactly this (Re:Zero ep-18 pattern) —
  give the confrontation room, then cash it in combat.
- Bottom out before payoff: the arc's floor beat (L85 in most villages) is what
  makes the finale's height. Never soften the floor.
- Epilogue beat: after each finale, one quiet scene of the new normal (Seat of
  Scars serves this game-wide; each village's post-finale herald copy is the
  mini version).

**Tone pendulum** (Kodaka/Ryukishi rule): never two dread scenes or two comedy
beats adjacent. The warm scene BEFORE the cruel one is load-bearing, written to
arm it, not filler. Tone shifts get a hinge line: a comedy scene ends on one
quiet wrong note; a dark scene ends on one small human joke. Registers by scene
type: comedy in preparation/downtime, warmth in aftermath, dread in
consequence, hype in decision-and-execution. No joking inside a payoff beat.

**Choice feel** (VLR/Slay-the-Princess/Telltale-derived):
- Write the options a player would actually WANT to do in that moment, not the
  options that are convenient to branch.
- DELAY THE WEIGHT: the biggest choices should read casual and land heavy in
  retrospect. No consequence-previews; the aftermath teaches the cost.
- Lopsided pick-rates are fine; spend the writing on acknowledgment, not
  branching. Players remember choices that get REFERENCED later (our trait
  callbacks), not choices that forked the plot.
- The memorable choice has no correct answer; if a walkthrough could rank the
  options, rewrite them.
- For the truly irreversible (the finale trio), escalate: one confirmation
  beat with mounting cost, so the player opted in twice and owns it.

**Companion craft** (Konosuba/Re:Zero/Key-derived):
- Each companion has ONE flaw pressed for comedy and ONE wound pressed for
  drama; new random quirks dilute, the same button freshly pressed compounds.
- Introduce at maximum archetype, crack it within two scenes.
- Companions argue STRATEGY, not just feelings — they propose competing plans
  the player must pick between; that is what makes them people with stakes.
- Companions have goals that exist without the player (the trophy-shelf cast
  is the genre's most-resented failure).
- Affection is banked in funny scenes and spent in sincere ones; the payoff
  scene only lands if the banking happened (Clannad economics).

**Village voice separation test:** cover the names; a Stormveil line should
smell of weather and bets, an Ashen Leaf line of sap and ash, a Frostfang line
of rosters and cold, a Moonshadow line of rates and masks. Kite Harrow is the
only character who speaks money in all four. Every named character gets 2-3
verbal tics enforced in review (Takumi: if you can swap nametags without
noticing, the scene is dead).

**Anti-patterns (hard bans):** meeting bloat (two consecutive seated-talking
scenes), lore walls (world rules revealed only at the moment of use, one rule
at a time), choice droughts (a full VN scene with zero player input), fake
choices (options that neither express character nor change anything), unearned
victory (wins without cost read as filler), atmospheric stalling before the
hook.

## 10. Ashen Leaf — Full Arc Rewrite (the pilot)

Structure notes: levels, boss names, lane trait strings, interlude trait
strings, and relationship traits (toma-hope / toma-caution / toma-doubt) are
all FROZEN (live save data / shipped art). Titles, page copy, scene captions,
and choice text are free. "First Flame" is re-canonized, not renamed: the
First Flame is the founders' willing fire; the Rootfire is what the harvest
made of it; the First Flame Avatar speaks for the willing dead.

### Chapters

**L4 — Roots of the Shinobi.** The player signs the Branch Register: not just
who you are — what you intend to become, whom you love, what you would build,
where you would go if you ever left. Toma, ahead in line, answers too smoothly,
like a recital. When the player signs, the Register blooms black along their
line (anchor glitch #1; the clerk has never seen that). Objective: the grove
trial (Wooden Root Guardian). Mid-scene choice: answer the becoming-question
honestly / give the recital answer Toma fed you / leave it blank and watch the
clerk's face. The invasiveness IS the scene.

**L15 — The Forbidden Seed.** Black flowers open along a family's fence
overnight. The village celebrates a blessing of the roots; Mori counts blooms
the way other men count graves — a bloom means an impossible future has
appeared nearby. One is on Toma's family fence. Objective: stand the bloom
watch; stop a mother trying to cut her own child's flower before the survey
sees it (she knows what a "blessing" costs). Boss: Rootbound Guard Initiate.

**L25 — The Cut Branches** (retitled from "Names Removed from Scrolls"). The
archive at night. Nothing is missing; that is the horror. Letters that go dull
mid-page. A carpenter's drawings that stop at the third line and resume as
furniture repair. Aren's page: present, complete, smaller. Toma's proof scene
(sample in §11). Boss: the Archive Spirit of the Root wakes when the player
pulls cut slats.

**L35 — The Rootfire Chamber** (retitled from "The First Flame Chamber").
Beneath the kiln: the founders' alcove, hand-carved offerings signed by the
willing dead; then the modern racks, stamped graft-slats, unsigned, fresh.
The First Flame Sentinel was built by the founders to guard the fire from
theft; Hoshina's crews walk past it with permits. Boss: First Flame Sentinel
(it cannot tell the player from a thief; the tragedy is it is RIGHT to be
suspicious of everyone else who came in this year).

**L50 — The Branch That Rises.** The Jonin rite is a graft. Measurements taken
like a cutting; the oath includes "I will grow where the tree permits." The
player realizes rank is not promotion; it is being grafted into rootstock.
Mid-scene choice: swear it plainly / ask what happens to branches that grow
past permission / stay silent and watch who flinches. Boss: Rootbound Master.

**L65 — The Mission of Quiet Ash.** Escort duty: crates of "seasoned
offerings" to the kiln. Toma opens one. Not plates — prototypes, letters,
seed jars, sketches, exit maps, love letters. The forbidden-futures shipment.
Costed trio: burn the manifest and scatter the crates now (the proof of
Hoshina's orders goes with it) / follow the shipment to the kiln to copy the
manifest while two crates go in / sign for the crates yourself and hold them
(now YOU are the custodian of other people's futures). Boss: Rootbound
Retrieval Squad.

**L75 — The Ancestors Speak.** The First Flame Avatar manifests, furious — not
that the dead are forgotten, but that the harvest is done in their name. "We
GAVE our green years. Gave." Boss: the Ancestor-Bound Flame Beast, a
grief-maddened remnant Hoshina chained to the kiln as a regulator. The willing
dead ask the player to be their witness, not their avenger.

**L85 — The Kage Burns the Future.** Center of the arc. The Detainment of the
Becoming: inventors, reformers, an apprentice too gifted, two forbidden
lovers, a child who draws machines — high-yield futures, scheduled for the
Rootfire before winter. Hoshina explains it herself (sample in §11). Costed
trio: free them tonight and the shipment manifest burns with the depot / trail
the crates for the manifest while two detainees go inside / claim custody "in
the Kage's name" and hold their futures as leverage. Boss: Rootbound Elder
Champion.

**L100 — The Tree Must Choose.** Hoshina, the shears, and the Vessel: the fire
wears her, ring by ring. Reckoning callbacks (interlude-trait gated, rewritten
to pruning canon: the lesson learned at Mori's side, the fate of Aren's proof,
what the player did with their own cut page). Final trio at the shears —
BREAK (trait `honorable`): futures flood back, families remember true, walls
crack, winter comes honest; BIND (trait `merciful`): willing offerings only,
and every cold year after is a referendum on your mercy; TAKE (trait
`ambitious`): the shears fit your hand, and the Register starts a fresh page
in your ink. Boss: Kage Hoshina Enju, First Flame Vessel.

### Interludes (levels/traits frozen; content recast)

- **L20 — The Unsworn.** Kite Harrow at the Register annex, hired to appraise
  a season's grafts for outside buyers. Her canon line survives: "Efficient.
  Most archives only eat paper." She is the first person to say out loud that
  the rite has a YIELD.
- **L30 — Aren's Handwriting** (was "Toma's Relic"). Toma shows the player the
  letter with the shaking ink and the water-screw junction that works. The
  relationship trio stays: promise him Aren's future gets finished someday

## 11. Sample Scenes (Ashen Leaf, new voice)

*(Three scenes, written to the light-novel register: clear, present, personal,
zero dashes, no accountant vocabulary. These are the audition for the whole
rewrite.)*

### Sample A — L4, the Branch Register (first page of the game's Ashen Leaf arc)

**Scene: The Register Hall, morning light through cedar smoke.**

**Toma Reed:** First time signing? Stand here, next to me. If you stand in the
middle the clerks think you have something to hide.

**Toma Reed:** It goes like this. Name. Family. Craft. Then the fourth
question. Everyone hesitates on the fourth question, so don't feel bad.

**Toma Reed:** "What do you intend to become." Just... answer smaller than the
truth. That's free advice. My brother taught me that.

**Narrator:** The Register is a wall of living cedar, forty strides long.
Every villager has a line in it. Some lines glitter with fresh ink. Some are
old and dark as roots.

**Narrator:** The clerk dips the quill for you. The hall is very quiet, the
way a forest is quiet when something large is walking through it.

**CHOICE:**
- Answer the fourth question honestly.
- Give the small, safe answer Toma fed you.
- Leave it blank and watch the clerk's face.

*(Whatever the player picks: as the ink dries, a black flower opens along
their line in the cedar. The clerk goes still. Toma stops smiling. "That,"
he says quietly, "usually takes years.")*

### Sample B — L25, Toma explains Aren (the arc's heart)

**Scene: The archive stacks, one lamp between them.**

**Toma Reed:** Here. Aren Reed. My brother. Go on, read it. Nothing is
missing. That's what I need you to understand first.

**Toma Reed:** Born in the ash-house row. Apprenticed at the joiner's bench.
Quiet. Dutiful. It even has his smile right. Everyone who reads this page
nods and says, yes, that was Aren.

**Toma Reed:** My mother remembers him like this page. That is the worst
part. She remembers him quiet. Dutiful. Happy at the bench.

**Toma Reed:** But I have his letter.

**Narrator:** He unwraps it like a wound. Cheap paper, close writing, and in
the middle of the second line the ink stutters, once, where a steady hand
shook.

**Toma Reed:** He was building a water-screw for the terrace fields. He filed
a complaint against the pruning rites. He wrote, right here: "If they cut me,
Toma, remember me arguing."

**Toma Reed:** The village did not erase my brother. It made him useful. It
went into my mother's head and it sanded him down and she thanks the roots
every year for such a peaceful son.

**Toma Reed:** So no. I am not protecting a name plate. Everyone in this
village keeps the names. I am keeping what he was BECOMING. And I need to know
if you're going to help me or report me, because I just handed you enough to
prune us both.

**CHOICE:**
- "Aren's future gets finished. I'll help you build it." (`toma-hope`)
- "I'll hold the letter. I promise nothing else yet." (`toma-caution`)
- "Burn it, Toma. Before it prunes you too." (`toma-doubt`)

### Sample C — L85, Hoshina explains pruning (the villain's best scene)

**Scene: The Kage's orchard office. Rain on new grafts.**

**Kage Hoshina Enju:** You have seen the detainment lists. You have come to
tell me they are monstrous. Sit. You will do it better sitting down.

**Kage Hoshina Enju:** Before you speak: the winter of the split oak. Forty
one dead. The granary winter, twenty six, most of them small. I was a girl
for the first and Kage for the second, and after the second, no child in
Ashen Leaf has frozen. Not one, in thirty years.

**Kage Hoshina Enju:** You believe I burn futures because I hate them. I have
read every future I ever cut. The boy who draws machines: his machines end
the terrace farms within ten years, and the farms feed four hundred. The two
lovers: their houses feud, and feuds here are buried with shovels.

**Kage Hoshina Enju:** Growth without pruning is rot. Every orchard keeper in
this village knows it. I am only the one who must know it about people.

**Kage Hoshina Enju:** So tell me, child of no recorded becoming. If you held
the shears, and the branch in front of you would drop the whole tree... would
you truly stay your hand? Or do you simply want me to be the one holding them
when it happens?

**CHOICE:**
- "Nobody should hold them. Not you. Not me."
- "Then the shears answer to law, not to one gardener."
- "Give them to me and find out."

## 12. Kite Harrow (unchanged role, sharpened function)

The unsworn broker who appears in all four arcs. She understands each system
because she stands outside all of them; no village ever gave her a place that
could not be revoked, so she prices everything, including herself. She is the
character who sees the FINANCIAL SHAPE of every moral failure, and the only
one licensed to speak money in every village (which is exactly why hearing it
from anyone else should feel wrong).

Each anchor tempts her with a version of belonging: a posted name, a line in
the Register, a place in the count, a file that says who she is. Her arc bends
to the player: pulled back from selling herself; partnered under dangerous
terms; or turned into someone who refuses to do business with what the player
has become.

## 13. Companions (the real stakes)

The player is not saving four systems. The player is saving:

- **Mira** from becoming the board (a person who only exists as posted fights
  and exit plans).
- **Toma** from becoming Mori (a good man who learns to read the flowers and
  starts triaging other people's futures).
- **Yura** from becoming Kael (a rescuer who seals every door she was once
  left outside of).
- **Nyx** from becoming Sable (a keeper who owns everyone she protects).

Each companion gets: one wound, pressed repeatedly; one scene where they are
funny or warm; one scene where they are wrong; one moment where the player's
accumulated choices decide which way they break.
